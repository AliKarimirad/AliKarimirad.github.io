About

I am an Assistant Teaching Professor in the Department of Economics at the University of Washington. My research studies how financial frictions and distortions propagate through production networks, with further work on trade infrastructure and migration policy.

I teach macroeconomics and financial economics from the introductory level through the Ph.D. program. Before joining UW, I was an economist with the Government of British Columbia, where I worked on trade policy and tariff analysis. I received my Ph.D. in Economics from the Vancouver School of Economics at the University of British Columbia in 2024.

Featured Research

Job Market Paper

Cheap Liquidity, Costly Default: Trade Credit in Production Networks

with Khalil Esmkhani

What does it cost the economy when firms finance intermediate inputs with cash up front or with credit from their suppliers? In the United States, defaultable trade credit costs 0.289% of consumption, and almost all of it comes from resources lost in bankruptcy and carried through the production network, not from misallocation.
